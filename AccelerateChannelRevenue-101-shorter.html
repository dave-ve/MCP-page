What's actually limiting your channel growth?

Rarely demand — usually the cost and effort of running each new channel. When agents operate the catalogue, that ceiling lifts. Three limits fall away:

The cost ceiling

Each new channel normally earns less than the last, until one costs more to run than it makes — with agents, that point moves far out.

The effort ceiling

Manual upkeep caps how many channels a team can run — agents carry the load, so it stops being a question of hours.

The break-even ceiling

Small markets have to clear a high setup bar — when setup is near-zero, the channels you used to skip become revenue worth taking.

Why can't my current PIM fix this?

Most were built to be run through a screen, so the work scales with the people doing the clicking. Bolting on AI doesn't change that — the limit is the architecture, not the interface. Commerce has already been through this shift once:

The part everyone did

Headless commerce

Splitting the storefront from the commerce engine, so any frontend can plug in. Proven, widespread — and only half the picture.

The part that's still missing

Headless, agent-first PIM

Your product data isn't locked behind a UI either — and it's built for agents, not just API calls. Team, channels and AI agents all work from the same source, through whatever interface fits.

Headless commerce decoupled the storefront. We decoupled the work from the screen — so agents, not just people, can run the catalogue.

What makes adding a channel easy here?

Reaching a new channel, market or language normally takes a wave of work — and that work, not the opportunity, caps how fast you expand. Agents take it on; your team guides them and handles the exceptions.

Three things make it easy:

No starting the data over

A new channel, region or language usually means redoing the data. Agents take that on, so the catalogue stays ready for:

  • A new marketplace or sales channel
  • A new market, language or region
  • An AI agent reading it directly
Supplier data without the bottleneck

Feeds arrive in whatever shape suppliers send them — PDFs, CSVs, EDI. Agents handle the intake:

  • Ingest each feed in the format it arrives
  • Map it to your data model
  • Enrich the gaps as they go

So adding ranges scales with the catalogue, not your team's hours.

Built for regulated markets

Requirements are already in the platform, with agents filling and validating them — so each market isn't a separate scramble:

  • EU Digital Product Passport
  • GS1 and industry standards
  • Market-specific data rules
